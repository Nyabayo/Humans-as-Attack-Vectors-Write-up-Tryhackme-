## Task 2: The Human Element

Humans are the easiest target for attackers due to their access to company systems and sensitive information. This task explains why attackers prefer targeting humans instead of trying to breach complex security measures.

### Why Humans Are Targeted:
- Attackers seek access to company systems or sensitive data.
- The weak links—employees—often fall for social engineering tactics.

#### Attack Example:
- **Breach Google account of HR manager** → Steal employee database.
- **Trick a wealthy person** → Run malware via web banking session.
- **Breach IT administrator's VPN** → Gain access to corporate network.

---

## Task 3: Attacks on Humans

Attacks targeting humans rely on manipulating their psychology—this is called **social engineering**. The key tactics of social engineering include:
- **Phishing Attacks**: Fake emails to steal login credentials. 
- **Malware Downloads**: Attacker tricks victims into downloading malicious software.
- **Deepfakes**: AI-generated videos used to impersonate trusted figures.
- **Impersonation**: Attackers pretend to be legitimate figures (like IT or HR) to deceive employees.

### Key Concepts:
- **Social Engineering**: Manipulating psychology to breach security.
- **Impersonation**: Attackers pretending to be trusted individuals.

---

## Task 4: Defending Humans

Defending humans from cyberattacks involves both **Mitigation** and **Detection**. **Mitigation** refers to preventative measures, while **Detection** focuses on identifying attacks that bypass mitigation.

### Mitigation Measures:
- **Anti-phishing solutions**: Block phishing emails before users notice.
- **Antivirus/EDR solutions**: Prevent malware from running on corporate systems.
- **Security Awareness Training**: Train employees to identify phishing and social engineering attempts.

### Detection:
SOC Analysts use tools to detect these attacks and respond swiftly when they bypass defenses.
